I've had this keyboard for eight months now so I think I can actually say something useful about it rather than the usual first-week enthusiasm.

Short version: good keyboard, bad software, and the price is silly.

The switches are the reason to buy it. They're linear, 45g, and they feel genuinely excellent — smooth all the way down, no scratchiness even now after what must be a couple million keystrokes. I've typed on a lot of boards and these are top three easily. Sound is a bit high-pitched out of the box but a sheet of foam under the plate fixed that in about ten minutes.

The build is fine. Aluminium case, heavy enough that it doesn't move, slight flex in the middle of the plate that I notice maybe once a week and that no normal person would care about.

Now the software. God. It's an Electron app that takes nine seconds to launch, requires an account — an account! to remap keys on a keyboard I own! — and it has lost my layout twice. Twice. Once after a firmware update that I didn't ask for and couldn't decline. I now keep my layout in a text file and re-enter it by hand when the app eats it, which is not a sentence anyone should have to write in 2024.

Battery: about three weeks with the lights off, four days with them on. The lights are pointless, turn them off.

Is it worth £200? No. Honestly no. There's a board at half the price with the same switches and firmware you can actually control. I like this one and I'm keeping it, but I got it on sale and I'd have been annoyed at full price.

Buy it if you specifically want these switches and you're prepared to fight the software. Otherwise look at the cheaper stuff first.
